# Biomedical Data Scientist — Interview Questions

A curated collection of interview questions (with answers) for **Biomedical Data Scientist** roles — a well-established and widely-hired title across academic medical centers, health systems, biotech and pharma companies, and health-tech companies, sitting at the intersection of clinical/health data (EHR, claims, registries), applied statistics and machine learning, biomedical domain knowledge (imaging, wearables, multi-omics), and the practical engineering discipline of getting a model into real, reliable use.

This role overlaps with but is meaningfully distinct from adjacent titles like Bioinformatician (which centers more specifically on genomics/sequencing pipeline work) and Precision Medicine Analyst (which centers more specifically on clinical genomics interpretation) — a Biomedical Data Scientist's center of gravity is generally the broader applied data science craft (study design, statistical modeling, machine learning, deployment and monitoring) applied across the full range of biomedical data types, with genomics being one data domain among several rather than the primary focus. The specific balance of skills expected varies by employer — a role at a health system leans heavily on EHR data and clinical epidemiology, a role at a digital health company may lean on wearable/sensor data and product-facing ML, and a role at a pharma company may lean on clinical trial and real-world evidence analysis — but the questions here span the common core that essentially any biomedical data science role draws on.

> **Disclaimer:** This is a community knowledge base, not a certification or official curriculum. Answers aim to be technically sound and interview-appropriate — concise, structured, demonstrating reasoning — not exhaustive textbook chapters. Tools, regulatory guidance, and best practices in this field evolve quickly; always verify current documentation and literature for anything used in real practice.

## 🧭 How to use this repo

- **Candidates:** Work through categories in order — clinical/biomedical data fundamentals (1) and statistics/causal inference (2) underpin everything else. Categories 4–6 cover the major biomedical data domains (EHR/real-world data, epidemiological study design, imaging/signal data) a role might specialize in, and category 7 (deployment/MLOps) is where a lot of otherwise-solid modeling work quietly falls apart in practice, so it deserves real attention regardless of how research- versus product-oriented a given role is.
- **Interviewers:** Pick 1–2 questions per category rather than exhausting every category in one loop, and weight categories to the specific role — a health-system-facing role should lean on categories 4–5, a product/ML-engineering-facing role should lean on categories 3 and 7. Category 10 (system design/case studies) is where practical judgment under real-world data and deployment constraints gets tested most directly.
- **Everyone:** Answers are written to show *reasoning* — why a particular causal inference assumption matters, why a model's strong offline performance doesn't guarantee real clinical utility — not just to state facts.
